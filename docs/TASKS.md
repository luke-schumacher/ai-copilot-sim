# Group 1 tasks: the simulator rig (7 students: 1 Master, 6 Bachelors)

Hours are for the whole group. Who: the squad that mainly does the task, or both squads.
Every student: 10 hours onboarding, 75 hours on the tasks below, 35 hours meetings, report and talk: 120 hours.
The labels (Master 1, Bachelor 1 and so on) only say who does how much; the group agrees in the first meeting who is which.
Start in week 1, without hardware: tasks 1, 3 (documentation and Luke's code first), 4 (method on laptops), 5 (mock stream), 6 (pipeline on mock data), 9 (dashboard on replayed data) and 10 (models on a laptop).
Group 2's tasks are in the `ai-copilot-lab` repository.

| # | task | what you do | in → out | done when | hours | who | depends on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Onboarding** | Get to know the plan for the rig, the rules and the repository. Run the mock data stream on your own laptop and read the setup guide once from start to finish. | In: repository, setup guide. Out: everyone has run the mock stream. | Everyone has run the mock stream and passes a short quiz on the data rule. | 70 (7×10) | Everyone | none |
| 2 | **Rig bring-up and setup guide** | Set up the delivered rig: the PC, wheel and pedals, the three simulators and the network. Correct and extend the setup guide as you go so that someone new can repeat it. Make a backup image of the PC. | In: delivered rig, setup guide. Out: rig in a known-good state, corrected guide, backup image. | Someone who has not touched the rig sets up all three simulators from the guide alone. | 60 | Rig squad | rig delivery (mid-November at the earliest) |
| 3 | **Game readers and game comparison** | Read speed, throttle, brake, steering and position from rFactor 2, Assetto Corsa and iRacing at a fixed rate. Luke hands over his existing reader code; you test it, fix it and extend it. Test each game on the rig or any Windows PC, then compare them on update rate, fields available, stability and effort to set up, and recommend which game or games to use for the test sessions. | In: Luke's reader code, documentation of the three games. Out: three working readers, a comparison table and a recommendation. | All three games record a 10-lap session without gaps, the real update rates are written down, and the comparison ends in a recommendation. Luke decides with you at a direction call. | 80 | Rig squad | none: start with the documentation and Luke's code |
| 4 | **Clocks and timestamps** | Every recorded value needs a time stamp you can trust. Measure how far the clocks of the simulator PC, the evaluation PC and the AI machine disagree and drift apart, and decide which time each value is stamped with. | In: the three machines on the switch. Out: timestamp policy, measurement script, results. | Offset and drift of all three machines measured over one hour, with the error stated. | 35 | Both squads | none: try the method on laptops first |
| 5 | **Data flow with ROS 2** | Send the telemetry from the simulator PC to the other machines with ROS 2, a toolkit for passing messages between programs. Define the message, record the stream and replay it with its original timing. | In: mock stream, later the real readers. Out: message definition, bridge, recording and replay. | A recorded stream replays with its original timing and time stamps. The message is agreed with Group 2 by 13 November. | 45 | Data squad | none: mock stream first |
| 6 | **Data pipeline** | Build the path the data takes after capture: store every session in one agreed layout, check its quality (gaps, jumps, impossible values), cut it into laps and make it easy to replay. The dashboard, the lap files and the AI work all build on it. | In: recordings from task 5. Out: stored, checked sessions and a quality report. | One command takes a recording to a checked, stored session, and a report lists every problem found. | 50 | Data squad | tasks 3 and 5 (start on mock data) |
| 7 | **Lap files and labelled sessions** | Convert stored sessions into the lap-file format that Group 2's checker reads and check them with Group 2's validator. Then drive at least 10 test sessions following Group 2's driving cards, for example 'brake 10 m earlier at turn 3', and label each one. | In: stored sessions, Group 2's driving cards and validator. Out: lap files and 10 labelled sessions. | 10 labelled sessions of 6 or more laps pass the validator and load in the checker. | 45 | Both squads | task 6; Group 2's tasks 2 and 7 |
| 8 | **Different setups and the delay model** | Run the chain in at least three setups, for example everything on one PC, simulator PC to evaluation PC, simulator PC to AI machine, and measure delay, jitter and lost data in each. Then build a SimPy model, a Python tool for simulating events over time, of those delays and check it against your measurements. | In: tasks 4 and 5. Out: measurements per setup, a calibrated delay model, value ranges for Group 2. | The model reproduces the measured median and 95th-percentile delay in two setups. The ranges are handed to Group 2. | 60 | Both squads | tasks 4 and 5 (start with assumed values) |
| 9 | **Interface tests and dashboard** | Test the interfaces between the parts (game reader, ROS 2, pipeline, lap files, Group 2's checker) and the data reporting. The test code for the interfaces and the reporting is already in the repositories: extend it. Then build a dashboard on the evaluation PC that pulls the data, runs Group 2's checker on it and shows the evaluation, with live and replay views. This dashboard is what you present at the end. | In: stored sessions from task 6, Group 2's checker and validator. Out: interface tests and a dashboard that pulls and evaluates the data. | The interface tests pass. The dashboard pulls stored and live data, shows the checker's answers against the known answers, and runs on the evaluation PC. | 55 | Both squads | task 6 (start with replayed mock data) |
| 10 | **Local AI: setting up and testing models** | Set up the AI machine and run local language models on it. Compare at least three models on the same tasks, for example turning a sentence into a structured test and summarising a lap. Measure speed and memory use and check the answers on ten example sentences. Group 2 runs the full accuracy study, so serve the models in a way they can call. | In: AI machine, candidate models. Out: model comparison (speed, memory, first check) and a running model server. | A written comparison of three models with measured speed and memory and a recommendation. The chosen model answers requests from the simulator PC and from Group 2. | 40 | Data squad | AI machine delivery (start on a laptop) |
| 11 | **Cameras, raw inputs and heart rate** | A later stage. Record two cameras with time stamps, read raw wheel and pedal data, and read the Polar H10 chest strap (heart rate, and an ECG trace, the heart's electrical signal). Align everything with the telemetry. | In: tasks 4 and 5, cameras, wheel base, pedals, chest strap. Out: aligned recordings of video, inputs and heart rate. | Each source is recorded and aligned with the telemetry, and the alignment error is stated. | 55 | Rig squad | tasks 4 and 5 |
| 12 | **Meetings, report and final talk** | Weekly team meetings, the weekly merge request, a short report and the final presentation. | In: all results. Out: report of at most 12 pages and a talk. | The report is reviewed by Luke and the talk has been rehearsed once. | 245 (7×35) | Everyone | all |
